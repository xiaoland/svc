# Coordination

Read this as an executing owner or consumer when an intermediate exception needs communication, a dependency needs early delivery, or effects overlap. It expands the entry's execution boundaries; establish what can proceed, what must wait, and who owns the affected work.

Explain what changed, why it cannot wait, and what decision or action is needed; combine related information. Continue authorized work that does not depend on a response. Use completion events or observations suited to the duration, uncertainty, and consequences, without per-step requests. Routine progress stays in the task record.

Before redirecting a continuing session, consult its current state and latest direct Human decisions: it may know a newer goal, constraint, or authorization. Preserve effective progress and resolve a real authority conflict instead of overwriting it with stale context.

Where authorized, dependency owners coordinate directly; the overall owner resolves cross-task priorities, consequential tradeoffs, and integration rather than relaying messages. Identify the usable revision or artifact, its observations, remaining work, and owner. The consumer freezes or qualifies the version it actually uses. Work in progress may not be stable enough for another owner's build or deployment; repeated rebuilding calls for repairing the delivery boundary.

Coordinate actual mutable targets before overlapping action. Different directories, checkouts, or machines can share services, databases, accounts, deployments, or submissions. Prefer one execution owner per target unless a safe concurrency boundary exists. Preserve others' changes and follow actual effect gates. A target change may invalidate earlier evidence; requalify the affected claim rather than everything by default.
