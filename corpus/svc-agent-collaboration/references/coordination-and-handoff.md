# Coordination and Handoff

Read this when continuing independent sessions, coordinating dependencies or shared effects, redirecting work, or replacing an owner. It expands the entry's continuity and responsibility guidance. Establish who continues the work, what is actually ready to consume, and what effects remain in flight; an update or intermediate artifact is not a responsibility transfer.

## Continue the Existing Owner

An independent session retains responsibility across intermediate steps and ordinary failures. Before redirecting it, consult its current task state and latest direct Human decisions: it may know a newer goal, constraint, or authorization than the caller. Preserve those decisions and effective progress, then send the relevant change with material entrances. Resolve a real conflict in goals or authority explicitly rather than overwriting one session with stale context. Session creation and messages follow the actual tool and project authorization.

Keep connected investigation, repair, preparation, operation, and verification with the same owner. For example, an authorized outcome of restoring and starting a run includes identifying its source, making necessary repairs, preparing the artifact, starting it, and reading back actual state, while existing experiment and effect limits still apply. Repeated routine escalations or waits for local instructions suggest inadequate authority or fragmented responsibility; repair the assignment or consolidate ownership before adding another Agent.

## Send Information That Changes Execution

Send a follow-up for a new Human decision or constraint, a material dependency becoming usable or blocked, a question that needs the recipient, or a result ready for adoption. Combine related updates and explain their consequence. Routine progress belongs in the task record and ordinary Human updates. Repeated urgency messages, unchanged permission reminders, and per-step acknowledgment requests consume attention without changing the work.

Use available completion events or an observation interval suited to the task's duration, uncertainty, and consequences. Let authorized execution continue between observations. Silence alone is neither failure nor evidence that effects stopped; inspect the relevant state when a concrete dependency or safety boundary requires it. Communicate whether work is assigned, executing, or supported by evidence without reproducing every local operation in the overall conversation.

Where messaging is authorized, dependency owners coordinate their engineering exchange directly. The overall task owner resolves cross-task priorities, consequential tradeoffs, and integration rather than relaying the same messages. Direct coordination does not expand either owner's authority or make the recipient's unsupported claim true.

## Deliver Something Actually Consumable

Identify what result, revision, or artifact is ready to use, the applicable observations, and any remaining dependency. A source edit in progress is not necessarily a stable input for another owner's build or deployment. Let the consumer freeze or qualify the actual version it will use once the needed boundary is ready. If unfinished shared edits repeatedly force rebuilding or refreezing, repair the delivery boundary or ownership instead of producing more status messages.

An intermediate delivery can unblock a consumer while its owner continues the remaining work. State what is usable and what remains owned so delivery does not accidentally discharge the larger outcome. Preserve evidence entrances and source identity sufficient to challenge the result, without requiring every message to reconstruct the history.

## Coordinate the Actual Mutable Targets

Identify overlapping edits and effects in the real environment. Separate contexts, checkouts, or machines do not isolate shared services, databases, accounts, deployments, or external submissions. Prefer one execution owner per mutable target unless the operation has an explicit safe merge or concurrency boundary. File ownership alone does not resolve conflicts over a shared run or external resource.

Coordinate before starting overlapping effects, preserve others' changes, and use the actual authorization and effect gates. Evidence obtained before another owner changed a target may no longer support the current candidate or state. Record the affected identity and conditions needed to decide whether it remains applicable; avoid global requalification without a concrete dependency.

## Transfer Responsibility Deliberately

Transfer when there is a reason to replace the owner, not merely because it delivered an artifact or encountered a recoverable failure. Preserve current Human decisions and authority, progress, evidence entrances, work in progress, remaining obligations, shared target ownership, and pending operations. Establish which owner accepts continuation and who handles each in-flight effect. The task record should make the change recoverable without creating competing authority or duplicate state.

After interruption or loss of contact, inspect the original work, saved artifacts, and relevant actual operations before assigning the same effects again. A missing reply does not establish cancellation; the operation may still execute after its Agent stops. Stop, wait for, or coordinate existing operations as required by the actual environment before a successor repeats them. If their state cannot be established, report that uncertainty and choose an authorized continuation that does not assume they ended.

Conclude the transfer when the successor and pending-effect responsibility are clear. Conclude the overall collaboration only when the result has been integrated or a bounded gap and next action are returned. Preserve the surrounding task's unmet obligations and inform the Human of material owner or completion-state changes.
