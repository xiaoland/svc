# Product Design

Use this Workflow design view to propose user behavior, interaction, and recovery. When the result's appearance is itself part of the outcome — visual authority, direction, and craft — [UI Design](ui-design.md) takes over from the behavior promises settled here. An interface's vocabulary is behavior language, not decoration: name each control by the action it performs, in the user's terms, and keep one action under one name through its whole flow. Return an experience the person can use toward the intended benefit, with choices and reasons. Apply the shared judgment through concrete occasions, sketches, and comparisons.

## Understand the Task and Intent

Reconstruct a concrete occasion: the trigger, intended result, information available, current actions, and difficulty. Use an existing report or observation; ask for missing personal facts when they change the proposal. Use the occasion to derive necessary behavior and distinguish it from Human-dependent preferences or optional additions.

Connect related requests through the person's whole activity. Identify which choice changes the meaning or usefulness of the others, and develop that choice first. Use the resulting behavior to organize the proposal and its scope.

Translate qualities such as calm, direct, playful, or trustworthy into choices about attention, memory, defaults, failure, and repetition. Judge the proposed experience against those consequences as well as functional completion.

## Model Meaning and Behavior

Identify what the person acts on, what it means, and what refers to it. Moving a document between collections might change membership, relocate it for everyone, or create a copy. Use alternative interactions to clarify identity, references, permissions, and reversibility. Settle the distinctions the chosen behavior depends on before committing to it.

Keep vocabulary consistent within its context. When the same word has different meanings, compare the underlying decisions and rules before imposing one definition. An order can be complete for fulfillment while payment remains unsettled. Define what each participant can promise and how the meanings relate.

To find lifecycle and permission boundaries, follow the person repeating, interrupting, or resuming the activity. Identify what should reset, what should persist, who causes each transition, and which effects are irreversible. Use those differences to challenge the object model. Preserve distinctions that affect Product decisions; merge those equivalent for these decisions.

## Walk the Interaction

Sketch where the person goes, which actions are available, and what each reveals or changes. Place a new capability among existing actions and judge how their meanings and availability interact. Walk from trigger to usable result, checking that the person can choose and tell what happened. Derive relevant repetition, cancellation, lost access, partial completion, and recovery from this flow. Describe the visible state and next useful action.

For a screen, let its main question and resulting action guide prominence, reading order, grouping, and controls. Preserve useful secondary content. Use realistic amounts and content density to expose interaction problems, and label invented examples.

Cut taglines, helper text, and secondary labels that compensate for weak hierarchy or layout; repair the underlying presentation instead. Keep information required for accessibility, safe action, or domain understanding. Judge consequential product changes by their combined effect on user, developer, and agent experience; do not trade one against the others without an explicit Human decision.

## Make Choices Tangible

Use the task and recovery paths above to compare consequential alternatives: what must the person know, repeat, wait for, and recover from? Keep content comparable and choose a representation for the uncertainty:

- An interaction outline exposes navigation, action meaning, and missing information.
- A visual comparison exposes hierarchy, grouping, density, and identity.
- A clickable or runnable prototype exposes timing, manipulation, and feedback.

Build on real content and the existing design system. Use differences in audience, subject, and setting to choose imagery, type, color, and tone where those choices matter. Bring Human-dependent preference or perception to the concrete comparison with a recommendation and reasons.

Return the proposed behavior, interaction and recovery decisions, rationale, and open Product choices.
