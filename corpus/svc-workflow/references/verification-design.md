# Verification Design

Use Verification Design when a proposed solution must clarify what should be judged, how its result can be observed, or what boundary makes relevant feedback possible. It returns the judgment-related view of the same solution described by [Design](design.md), not a final test phase. The independent `svc-verification` Skill owns specialist criteria, evidence, and feedback reasoning; discover its installed entry by name through the host when that guidance is needed.

Start from the product result and the behavior constraints owned by the proposed solution. Identify the relevant claim, conditions, observation, and judgment rule, preserving assumptions that could break their connection. An unowned expected outcome is a requirement gap to resolve; tests and implementation should not manufacture the product promise. Bring an unresolved interpretation or observation gap into the solution rather than silently treating it as established.

Consider the observation boundary while shaping the solution. A success prompt does not establish that changed settings survive another session; if the promised result requires persistence and identity, make those relations observable under relevant conditions. A controllable clock, explicit state ownership, isolated test data, a real dependency boundary, or an inspectable outcome may materially change the technical arrangement. Choose such facilities for the claim they enable, not to expose private implementation details as permanent product expectations.

When requirement interpretation, condition selection, dependencies, measurement, reproducibility, evidence reuse, or feedback timing affect the judgment, seek the relevant guidance in `svc-verification` when available. It supplies additional reasoning rather than a required installation dependency. Without it, use qualified project mechanisms to obtain useful product-related feedback, preserve the basic requirement-to-observation relationship, and explicitly return any actual judgment or capability gap. Design remains responsible for the consequences those gaps have on the current solution.

Return the observations and boundaries needed to judge the current implementation horizon, their connection to the owned requirements, and material assumptions or gaps. Preserve the distinction between proposed checks and obtained evidence. Designing a plausible observation does not establish that behavior occurred or that the requirement serves the intended use; those decisions may need V&V during design, implementation, or result judgment.
