# Form and Revise Judgments

Use this Workflow reference to develop the shared judgment actions for a changed behavior, responsibility, problem framing, or route. The examples show how to find a consequential choice, reason through its effects, and revise it. Return a recommendation and the basis needed by the current work.

## Find the Decision inside the Request

Reconstruct a concrete use: what prompted the request, what the person or caller is trying to accomplish, the current difficulty, and the useful outcome. Separate the intended benefit, necessary behavior, suggested means, and inherited implementation. A mechanism explicitly required by the Human remains a constraint.

Connect requests that affect the same promise or depend on the same choice. Ask what would change elsewhere if this question had a different answer. Work on those determining choices before dividing the result into edits. A familiar function or UI control is a place to investigate; derive the solution boundary from the use and its dependencies.

For example, “continue work from my phone” first raises a question about where the work continues. Controlling the existing execution preserves its tools, files, and active state. Moving execution requires transferring those resources and deciding what happens to work still running on the original machine. A mobile screen and transport depend on that choice.

If the purpose is to inspect and steer work while the workstation remains available, control may be the simpler route. If work must continue after that workstation is turned off, migration or another execution host becomes relevant. Investigate resource access and actual runtime capabilities; bring the consequential availability preference to the Human with a recommendation. This produces a useful question and candidate routes before an implementation plan.

## Derive the Relations That Make the Promise Hold

Follow an important action through acceptance, its result, and a relevant later action or recovery. Vary something the person can normally change. Identify which facts must move together, which must persist independently, and who can make those conditions hold. Use these differences to choose identities, lifetimes, responsibility boundaries, and scope.

Consider an editor that can move from a document pane into a window-wide panel. Switching the target document should change where a send goes; the independent draft should remain. Hiding the panel changes visibility while preserving its choices. These differences imply separate ownership of draft and target, with an explicit pairing at the action. A view callback that installs an override into the target's store makes the promise depend on the view being present. Walking “hide → switch target → choose through a command → show” exposes that dependency before implementation.

The same reasoning distinguishes display association from control lifetime. Automatic retries belong to the current explicit operation and share its configuration and budget. After exhaustion, a Human may explicitly continue: the displayed work can remain associated while a new operation captures current choices and initializes a new budget. A single identity serving both lifetimes makes those rules conflict. Derive the necessary distinction from what resets and what persists, then choose its representation.

These distinctions depend on the consumer's promise. A deliberately limited common capability can share a model. Full native operation may need additional information. Compare what each consumer must preserve before choosing a common representation.

## Compare a Use and a Credible Change

Retain the purpose and actual obligations while varying a candidate's premise, responsibility, or mechanism. Include the current arrangement, reuse, or removal when credible. Use an established suitable choice directly when its reasons still hold.

Walk the candidates through the same use. First check their promises; then compare experience, failure, operation, transition, and future change. Make maintenance cost concrete: for a likely additional consumer or changed policy, identify who needs new knowledge, which owners must change together, and where exceptions or coordination accumulate. Compare that cost with the burden of a new boundary or abstraction.

Recommend once the evidence and reasoning are adequate for the current decision. State decisive reasons, costs, and assumptions that could change the choice. Investigate an unknown for the difference it could make to that decision. When actual use is needed, choose an authorized reversible increment and keep dependent commitments proportionate to what it can teach.

## Challenge a Visual Direction

A visual design plan passes review only when it is specific to its brief. After writing the plan, substitute a similar request with a different subject and ask whether the same plan would come out: palette, type, layout concept. If it would, the free axes were spent on defaults rather than decisions — revise and state what changed. The Human's explicit aesthetic direction overrides this check; the check exists for the axes the brief leaves open.

## Use Feedback to Reselect

Exercise a condition that distinguishes the explanation from a credible wrong one while constructing the result. In the phone example, disconnecting the original host tests an availability promise; a successful screen interaction alone leaves it unresolved. In the editor example, hiding the view tests whether ownership survives independently of presentation.

When results differ, check the observation and compare a local defect, an unsuitable mechanism or responsibility, and an inadequate problem framing. Repair a supported local cause directly. A changed purpose or better explanation can justify reselecting before repeated failures accumulate. Revisit factual premises behind accepted choices as evidence changes; bring changes to Human-owned goals, preferences, and material scope back for judgment.

Propagate the revised choice through affected concepts, contracts, consumers, criteria, plans, and implementation. Inspect related paths relying on the same premise. Withdraw restrictions, adapters, compensation, and checks that lost their basis, preserving independent uses, valid progress, and applicable evidence. Continue unaffected authorized work.

For a clear local correction, use the existing explanation: if a label reads the wrong already-owned value, fix that mapping and observe the relevant result. Let the consequences determine the depth of judgment and the amount of recorded reasoning.
