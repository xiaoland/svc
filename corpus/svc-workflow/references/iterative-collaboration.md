# Iterative Collaboration

Read this when product experience and new feedback continuously shape the current scope or route, or when an increment needs to be useful without freezing the requirements. It expands the workflow's feedback guidance. Return a consumable increment, investigation, or proposal with its evidence, experience entry when relevant, and unresolved choices; iteration does not require a fixed sprint, backlog, or ceremony.

Iterative collaboration keeps the goal and authority shared while revising how the next useful result will be reached. It is valuable when actual use can expose needs that an upfront description cannot settle. Starting implementation establishes a current route, not permanent correctness of the requirements. This flexibility still needs coherent choices and known effect boundaries; it does not authorize every suggestion or make rapid code changes the definition of progress.

Recover the current goal, latest feedback and decisions, authorization, usable state, and remaining uncertainty. Choose a bounded next result that can advance the goal or reveal a consequential distinction. It may be an investigation, a design comparison, or a realized behavior. Form enough of the solution to avoid silently inventing material requirements, plan a limited route, implement when authorized, and obtain evidence related to the product purpose. Use the methods needed for that gap rather than repeating a full method sequence each time.

Deliver a change the consumer can actually use and distinguish what is implemented, what its observations support, and what remains uncertain. Where preference, comprehension, or interaction experience matters, offer a concrete experience entry and a focused reason to examine it. The Agent remains responsible for feasible engineering and product-behavior observations; the Human supplies judgments that cannot be settled by a trustworthy oracle, rather than becoming the only test executor.

Interpret feedback before changing the route. A report that an already promised behavior fails calls for repair within existing authority. A proposed interaction may reveal a preference, a broader goal, or an unresolved tradeoff; inspect its consequences and recommend a choice instead of automatically installing it as a requirement. A contradiction in a key premise calls for local reconsideration of the requirement, design, or criterion. Pause only actions depending on that unresolved premise and continue other authorized work.

For example, if a changed setting disappears on the next login despite an agreed persistence requirement, reproduce the relevant path, inspect the oracle and observation, and repair the defect. If feedback instead suggests sharing the setting between accounts, clarify the intended ownership and privacy tradeoff before implementing that change. Both can occur during the same ongoing work, but they have different implications for the product promise and authority.

Update the current understanding and plan when the feedback changes them, preserving which conclusions remain supported. A prototype may clarify a new requirement without immediately becoming production behavior. A local design review does not by itself switch the whole project to staged collaboration; a change in the overall confirmation rhythm should remain shared with the Human. Do not send every routine engineering choice back for approval, or treat a period without feedback as acceptance.

Stop an increment when its intended return and relevant evidence are ready, then integrate them and identify the next useful gap if the task continues. If authority, environment, or an unsettled Human decision prevents further progress, return the supported state and smallest missing prerequisite. Finish the task only when the agreed result and remaining obligations have been addressed; continued iteration is not a reason to leave necessary authorized work unfinished.
