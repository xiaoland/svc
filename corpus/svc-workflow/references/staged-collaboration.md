# Staged Collaboration

Read this when work proceeds through reviewable commitments, when a proposal must be ready for a Human decision, or when feedback may invalidate an agreed premise. It expands the workflow's commitment guidance. Return what the current stage's consumer can review or use, with its evidence and remaining decisions; a stage is not a mandatory document or proof of overall completion.

Staged collaboration makes the current commitment explicit before dependent work advances. This can help when the Human wants to review scope or a consequential arrangement before investing in realization. Agree enough about the current goal, scope, key choices, and judgment basis to make that commitment meaningful. Do not require a complete design of the whole project, and do not create approvals for ordinary engineering decisions already covered by the agreement.

Start from the latest agreed result, authorization, known state, and open question. Investigate the uncertainty that could change a proposal, then present a coherent recommendation with decisive reasons, consequences, evidence entrances, and any material alternative. The Human should be deciding a meaningful choice, not supplying steps the Agent could determine. Use the workflow's methods as needed within any stage; investigation, design, implementation, and V&V may recur rather than occupy exclusive slots.

A proposal being understood, preferred, or confirmed does not automatically authorize every operation needed to realize it. Establish the actual implementation or effect authority before acting. Conversely, explicit authorization for an agreed implementation covers its necessary ordinary work unless the real project or tool boundary says otherwise; a method change or stage boundary does not revoke it. Finish concrete, reviewable preparation before asking for a decision that gates the next effect.

When feedback arrives, determine whether it reports failure to realize the existing promise, corrects an observation, proposes a new promise, or exposes an invalid premise. If the agreed behavior is simply missing and repair remains authorized, repair it and obtain relevant feedback. For example, an agreed setting that disappears after a new session needs persistence repair and an observation of that behavior, not a new product approval because the Human noticed it.

If the Human instead questions whether the setting should persist across users, that may alter identity, privacy, or the product promise. Identify which current commitment depends on the unsettled choice, pause that portion, and develop a decision-ready revision. Preserve valid work and continue independent authorized parts. Reopen the affected commitment rather than treating the entire plan as invalid or silently applying a newly assumed requirement.

At the current boundary, deliver the proposal, realized change, or supported judgment the consumer needs. Distinguish agreed intention, actual effect, evidence of behavior, and acceptance. A stage approved for implementation is not a verified product result; a passing check is not Human preference or acceptance. When experience matters, provide an actual usable entry and explain what the Agent has already examined, leaving the Human a meaningful judgment rather than all verification work.

If the Human is unavailable, preserve the decision and its impact, continue independent authorized work, and return the usable state with the unresolved acceptance or scope item. End the stage when its consumer has the supported return needed to continue, or return a bounded gap with the smallest next decision. The surrounding task retains all unmet obligations, including any later integration or result judgment.
